How I write a caption in four minutes.

If captions take you twenty minutes and three rewrites, it isn't you. It's the ask.

MegSeptember 17

Most caption advice tells you to write more. Write ten, pick the best one, test them all. That's the advice that keeps you in the app at 9pm with a reel you already filmed and still haven't posted.

The fix is the opposite. Ask for less, and tell it who you are first.

Why the captions you get back don't sound like you

When you type "write me a caption," Claude has nothing to go on except every caption on the internet. So it gives you the average of all of them. Polished, tidy, and not yours.

It isn't a Claude problem. It's a briefing problem, and it takes about ten minutes to fix once.

Short on time? Everything below is the free version. The skill I actually use does all of it for you in one line.

Step 1. Teach it your voice, one time

Open a new chat and paste this. Answer like you're texting a friend, because messy is fine.

paste this
I want you to learn my voice for Instagram captions. Ask me these three questions, one at a time, and wait for my answer before the next one. 1. Who is this post for? 2. I'll paste two or three captions that sound like me. 3. What do I never want in a caption?

The captions you paste teach it more than anything you can describe. Pick the ones that felt the most like you, not the ones that performed best. Those are two different things, and the second one will make you sound like everyone else.

Step 2. Ask for one caption, not ten

then this
Caption this: [your post in a sentence]. Give me one caption in my voice. Put the hook on the first line. Add something my video doesn't already say. End with one clear ask.

Ten options put you back in the chair as the editor. One caption makes you the approver, and approving takes four minutes.

Ten options means you become the editor. One caption means you're the approver.

Step 3. The three lines that make a caption work

The hook. The first line is all anyone reads before they tap more. It should name the feeling, not the topic.

The turn. One thing your video didn't already say. If the caption repeats the reel, there's no reason to read it.

The ask. One. Comment a word, save it, or send it to a friend. Two asks is the same as none.

What to do when it comes back wrong

Don't rewrite it yourself. That's the trap, and it's how a four-minute job turns back into twenty. Say what's off in your own words: "too polished," "that's not how I'd say it," "cut the first line." It learns from the correction and the next one lands closer.
