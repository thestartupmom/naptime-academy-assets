What I typed to get this reel

After the rough cut, the engine hands me my reel line by line. This is exactly what I wrote on each line, cleaned up so it makes sense, with a breakdown of every instruction so you can steal it.

I asked to see stills first. Before it built anything, I told Claude to send me a still picture of every graphic. A still shows up in seconds. The full moving version takes a while and uses up more tokens (your Claude usage). So I looked at the stills, said yes or asked for changes, and only then did it animate. I never waited on something I did not want.

Step 4 · the style plan

Every line, and what I told it

Thirty-one lines. I gave directions on eighteen of them. The rest get my normal caption and nothing else, which is on purpose. A reel where every line does something is a reel where nothing stands out.

This reel runs on my own style pack. Buyers make theirs by styling the Canva Style Pack that comes with the Reels Editing Engine, then telling it “read my Canva style pack.” From then on, every reel carries your look without you asking.

1We learned how to yap.
Note
Add my photo above my finger, behind my head, tilted a little to the right. Slide it in from the left with a floaty feel and hold it until line 3. Slide it out to the right at the same time as the other photo. Round the corners and add a soft drop shadow.
Why I said this
  • Behind my head. The engine cuts you out of your own footage, so the photo sits between you and the background. It looks like it is floating in your room.
  • Floaty. A slow, gentle drift while the photo holds, so it never sits frozen.
  • Hold until line 3. Name the line it should leave on, not a time in seconds. The engine does the math, and it still lines up if you trim later.

Why I said this
  • Mirror it. Describe the first one fully, then say “same, but flipped.” Opposite tilt, opposite directions in and out.
  • “At the same time as the other photo.” Tying two elements together keeps them moving as a pair, even though they came in on different lines.

5No, seriously.
Note
Keep “no, seriously” together as one caption. Both words bounce in at the same time with Cheeky Bounce, 30% smaller than my normal captions.
Why I said this
  • One caption, not two. By default each word can pop on its own. Saying “keep it together” makes the phrase land as one beat.
  • Cheeky Bounce. A named text animation. You can call any animation by name, and the effects library shows you all of them.
  • 30% smaller. A smaller caption reads like an aside. Size is how you set tone without changing a word.

The finish

Two more lines and it was done

    I told Claude, be my sound designer

    Once the picture was right, I handed off the sound. It pulls from the sound palette I built in CapCut and lines each sound up with the moment it belongs to, so a photo sliding in actually sounds like it slid in. The music bed I picked myself, like I said on line 6.

    Claude
